A derived cut is cheap; what it carries is not. On the Harness work, the class structure survived every cut placement inside its gap and ignored cuts outside it - but the load-bearing numbers were the two ends of the distribution: the control's chance nearest-neighbour ratio (1.987) and the weakest real separation (2.46). The whole method lives in that strip, so any claim built on a threshold must report the distance between the noise ceiling and the weakest signal, not just its own pass. Second lesson from the same run: a null result can be a coin toss - the identity probe sat at 0.400 against a gap high side of 0.4016, so 'unnamed' depended on where the midpoint landed. Place a non-membership probe clearly outside the boundary, and sweep the boundary to prove it.
